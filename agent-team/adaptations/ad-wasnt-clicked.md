# Adaptation · why the ad was not clicked

Question shape: "the **\<campaign\>** ad got **\<X\>** impressions, the click rate stayed at **\<Y%\>**, and it spent **\<Z\>**. The previous ad on the same account was getting **\<W%\>**. Why was it not clicked."

One campaign, one question.

## Four theories

| Role | Theory |
|---|---|
| **Marketer** | The image and the first line did not stop anyone. The ad looked like an ad in the feed and could not break the scroll reflex. The promise was not in the first three words. |
| **Editor** | A production problem. The image was cropped, the text was unreadable, the copy overflowed on mobile, the resolution was low, the aspect ratio was wrong. The message was fine, it just did not sit properly on screen. |
| **Skeptic** | Targeting and auction conditions. The audience is wrong, the frequency is too high, or the unit cost rose in that period. Nothing to do with the ad itself. |
| **Psychologist** | The cost of clicking looked high. The person understood the ad but did not click because they did not know what would happen next. The following step was unclear or asked for too much commitment. |

The Mathematician has no theory. Do not change it.

## Data fields the Mathematician will look at

**Campaign numbers**
- Impressions, reach, clicks, click rate
- Cost per thousand impressions and cost per click
- Spend and time on air
- Frequency (average impressions per person)

**Creative breakdown**
- Impressions and click rate for each image or video separately
- For video, three-second and completion rates
- Placement breakdown (feed, stories, discovery) with click rate for each
- Aspect ratio and amount of text on the image

**Comparison**
- The same measures for the account's last five campaigns
- The result of an ad shown to the same audience earlier
- Which measure changed and which stayed flat

**Audience**
- Targeting definition and audience size
- How it differs from the previous campaign's targeting
- Audience overlap share, if any
- Click rate by age, device and region

**After the click**
- How many of the clickers actually opened the page (the gap between clicks and sessions)
- Whether the promise in the ad matches the headline on the page that opens
- What action the ad was asking for

## The numbers that separate them

- Cost per thousand flat and click rate down puts you on the **Marketer** or **Psychologist** line, and weakens the **Skeptic**.
- Cost per thousand markedly up, or frequency above three, strengthens the **Skeptic**.
- Click rate swinging hard by placement (normal in one, bottom in another) strengthens the **Editor**: there is a cropping or ratio problem.
- High three-second video rate with low clicks strengthens the **Psychologist**: they watched, they did not click.
