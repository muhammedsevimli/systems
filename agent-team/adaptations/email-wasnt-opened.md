# Adaptation · why the email was not opened

Question shape: "the email I sent on **\<date\>** reached **\<X\>** people and the open rate stayed at **\<Y%\>**. Previous emails were getting **\<Z%\>**. Why did it drop."

One send, one question.

## Four theories

| Role | Theory |
|---|---|
| **Marketer** | The subject line did not work. The only things visible in an inbox are the sender name and the subject line. This one built neither curiosity nor benefit, and it was unlike the subject lines that came before it. |
| **Editor** | The email landed in the wrong place technically. Sender address, authentication records, image weight, link count or spam-triggering words kept it out of the primary view. It was not opened because it was not seen. |
| **Skeptic** | The list changed, the email did not. Subscribers who joined recently came from a different source and have a weak relationship with this brand. Or the send time, day or cadence changed. Nothing to do with the content. |
| **Psychologist** | Subscribers already know what is coming. Someone who did not find what they expected in the previous emails skipped this one without opening it. The decline started before this send, not in it. |

The Mathematician has no theory. Do not change it.

## Data fields the Mathematician will look at

**This send**
- Sent, delivered, opened, clicked counts
- Open rate and click rate
- Unsubscribes and spam complaints
- Send day, send hour, and the subject line itself

**Historical comparison**
- The same measures for the last six to ten sends, in date order
- The subject line of each
- Which send the rate broke on

**Deliverability health**
- Hard and soft bounce counts
- Status of the authentication records
- Image count, link count and total size of the email
- Tab or folder breakdown if available

**List segments**
- Breakdown by subscriber source, with open rate for each
- Breakdown by subscriber age (last 30 days, 30-90 days, over 90 days)
- Share who have opened nothing in 90 days
- How the list size changed over the last three months

**Behavior**
- Share who open every send (the loyal core)
- Those who clicked and did not come back
- The gap between what previous sends promised and what they delivered

## The numbers that separate them

- Deliverability measures flat and the rate down on a single send strengthens the **Marketer**.
- Bounces up or an authentication record broken strengthens the **Editor**: this is a delivery problem.
- A drop confined to one subscriber source or to new subscribers strengthens the **Skeptic**.
- A gradual decline across several sends, with the loyal core shrinking, strengthens the **Psychologist**.
