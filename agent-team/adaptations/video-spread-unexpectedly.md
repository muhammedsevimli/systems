# Adaptation · why the video spread

Question shape: "the short video I posted on **\<date\>** got **\<X\>** views, and the previous episodes were stalling around **\<Y\>**. Why did it spread."

One video, one question. The same shape works for a video that came in far below expectations; the direction just flips.

## Four theories

| Role | Theory |
|---|---|
| **Marketer** | The opening scene spread it. The first second, the cover frame and the opening line stopped people, and everything else rode on top of that. |
| **Editor** | The edit spread it. Cut pacing, where the music enters, caption rhythm and audio hygiene kept people on screen. |
| **Skeptic** | The existing audience spread it. Followers and their shares built the first wave, and distribution rode on top of that. Nothing to do with the video itself. |
| **Psychologist** | Viewer intent spread it. The viewer thought "I could do that", saved it to own it, and commented to ask for it. That behavior pushed the video into distribution. |

The Mathematician has no theory. Do not change it.

## Data fields the Mathematician will look at

The lead asks you for these in chat and writes `data/DATA.md` itself. Anything you do not have is written as "no data".

**Performance (24 hours after posting)**
- Views, unique viewers
- Non-follower share of viewers
- Likes, comments, saves, shares
- New follows
- Average watch time and video length

**Content context**
- What the first second showed (talking to camera, a screen recording, a piece of footage) and what the opening line was
- What the subject was and what the closing asked the viewer to do
- What most of the comments are: requests, compliments, or off topic

**Production measurements**
- Mix level, music to speech separation
- Cut count, dropped caption frames, full black frames

**External factors**
- Day and time of posting
- Whether it was boosted with paid promotion
- Whether a large account shared it, or any known external share
- Follower count that week

**Comparison (the critical part)**
Give the same list for at least one, preferably two more episodes:
- One that landed in the expected range
- If you have one, an episode built with the same opening format that got a different result

With that second comparison the referee can separate the packaging theory with arithmetic. Without it the referee writes "cannot be separated", and that is an honest outcome.

## The numbers that separate them

- If episodes sharing an opening format got similar reach while a different opening sat at the bottom, the **Marketer** strengthens.
- If production measurements stay flat across episodes, the **Editor** weakens; a constant variable cannot produce a change.
- A high non-follower share weakens the **Skeptic**. The arithmetic: unique viewers x follower share, then ask whether that number could carry the total.
- Saves and comments running at multiples of likes, and making up most of total engagement, strengthens the **Psychologist**.
- Weak saves and comments alongside strong reach cuts the **Psychologist**: if the signal is weak while reach is strong, that signal is not carrying reach.
