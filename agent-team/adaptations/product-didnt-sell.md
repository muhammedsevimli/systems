# Adaptation · why the product did not sell

Question shape: "**\<product\>** launched, **\<X\>** people saw the page, **\<Y\>** people bought. Why did it stay this low."

One outcome, one question. Do not ask "why is our product not selling", that is not a question. Ask about a specific launch, a specific window.

## Four theories

| Role | Theory |
|---|---|
| **Marketer** | The promise was built wrong. Whoever landed on the page did not understand what the product does on the first screen; the headline and opening paragraph promised the wrong thing. The message that brought the traffic and the message on the page were not the same. |
| **Editor** | The page itself blocked it. Load time, mobile layout, form length, friction in the payment step. The person was convinced and the flow dropped them. |
| **Skeptic** | The wrong audience arrived. A traffic source with no purchase intent filled the page, which is why the conversion rate fell. Nothing to do with the product or the page. |
| **Psychologist** | Risk stopped them, not price. The person wanted the product but could not resolve "will this work for me, will I lose my money". There was no refund, no trial, no proof, no sample output. |

The Mathematician has no theory. Do not change it.

## Data fields the Mathematician will look at

The lead asks you for these in chat and writes `data/DATA.md` itself. Anything you do not have is written as "no data", so the referee marks the gap as a gap.

**Funnel numbers**
- Page views, unique visitors
- Count reaching the checkout page
- Count starting payment
- Count completing
- Drop rate at each step

**Page behavior**
- Average time on page
- Scroll depth (share who left on the first screen)
- Mobile and desktop conversion separately
- Page load time

**Traffic source**
- Breakdown by source, visitors and sales for each
- Average time on page per source
- Returning visitor share

**Comparison**
- An earlier launch of the same product, or a similar product on the same page structure
- The price, promise and page structure used in that launch

**Intent signals**
- Count who added to cart and did not proceed to payment
- Number of inbound questions and emails, and what they were about
- Was a refund policy on the page, was there a proof section

## The numbers that separate them

- Short time on page and shallow scroll strengthens the **Marketer**: they left before the message landed.
- Deep scroll and high checkout entry but low completion strengthens the **Editor**: the flow dropped them.
- If one traffic source converts markedly worse than the others and carries real volume, the **Skeptic** strengthens.
- High add-to-cart, low completion, and inbound questions mostly asking "will this work for me" strengthens the **Psychologist**.
