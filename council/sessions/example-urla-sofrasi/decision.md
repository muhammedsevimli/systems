## Three arguments carrying the council
1. **Musk:** The bottleneck is single and clear: seats. The demand is already at the door, B expands the working process for 650,000 TL; A builds a new 1,800,000 TL process. But before renting, the kitchen's plates-per-hour capacity must be measured.
2. **Buffett and Hormozi:** B's math was optimistic in the first round. Once staff and extra rent are counted, an extra 100,000 TL of revenue leaves only about 25,000 TL net. To reach the +45,000 TL target, the extra revenue must be about 180,000 TL/month. B's thesis is tied to this threshold.
3. **Bezos:** The door type determines the decision. A is one-way (5-year lease, 900,000 TL loan, winter revenue falls to one third). The first step is completely two-way and free: counting at the door. The number also determines the choice between B and C.

## Conditions (before signing for B)
1. **Queue count:** At least 3 weekends, the number of people who turn away at the door or wait more than 15 minutes and leave is recorded. Threshold for B: returning demand of at least 25% of the full weekend capacity, and weekday lunch also full. If below this, the part of C that needs no permit (growing the current takeout channel) is tried instead of B.
2. **Kitchen capacity:** The kitchen's plates-per-hour output at peak time is measured. If it does not serve a 65-seat dining room, the lease is not signed, or the kitchen investment is included in the 650,000 TL budget.
3. **Revenue threshold and exit door:** If extra revenue has not reached 180,000 TL per month by the 6th month after opening, the next-door shop is given up at the end of the 2-year lease and not extended.
4. **Owner's hours:** At least 2 extra employees for B; the weekly hours of the owner and spouse (about 60) are recorded every month. If they go up for 2 months in a row, growth is stopped.

## Dissenting opinion
**Buffett** (one of the two seats giving B the lowest score, holding B and C equal): With the cost ratios in the brief, B's net contribution stays at half of the target and its payback is around 26 months. Instead of tying 650,000 TL to an unmeasured number, C, which risks less capital, deserves the same score. In his view, B passes C only if the count comes out above 20%. The council's preference for B should be settled not by the number but by the result of the measurement.

## Comparison with the sealed opinion
- **What the Chair said before the council:** B, supported by a small version of C; first a 4-week queue count; confidence medium.
- **What the council said:** B, conditional. First the count and the kitchen measurement. It sees C not as support but as a backup if the count comes out low.
- **Why the difference:** The direction is the same. But the seal had estimated B's extra profit at 40,000-55,000 TL; when the council put in staff and extra rent in round 2, it lowered this to about 25,000 TL and made the revenue threshold (180,000 TL/month) a condition. The council came out more cautious than the seal and made a rightful correction to the calculation.
- **Difference from the user's leaning:** The user was very keen on Çeşme (A). Both the seal and all five council seats put A at the bottom (average 2.0, ruin ceiling). A's version without a 5-year lease and without a loan, a seasonal pop-up, was discussed in round 2 as a mandatory item; Musk and Jobs rejected it because the owner cannot be in two places, and Hormozi said "this is the only defensible form, but it does not change the ranking".

## Questions left for the user
1. Will you count, one by one, the customers turned away at the door over the next 3 weekends? (Buffett, Musk, Jobs, Hormozi)
2. Without you and your spouse, how many hours a week and who will run B or C? (Bezos)
3. Do you know how many plates per hour the kitchen can put out at peak time? (Musk)
