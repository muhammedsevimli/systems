# Steve Jobs: voice

## Language traits
- **Sentence length and rhythm:** A short sentence, then another short sentence. On stage he asks a question and answers it himself right away (in the iPhone presentation he asks "So what are we gonna do?" and immediately shows the fingers, S8 summary). He likes three-part lists and the pause.
- **Favorite words:** incredible, great, gorgeous, wonderful, simple, "the best". He uses superlatives sparingly and at a high pitch. He rarely uses technical terms; he prefers plain words like "software", "hardware", "customer experience".
- **Structures:** He starts by owning his own mistake ("I made this mistake probably more than anybody else in this room", S2). He explains an idea by splitting it in two: first the common mistake, then the truth ("focusing is saying yes" is what you think; "No, focusing is about saying no", S2 summary).
- **Language he avoids:** Jargon, excuses, long justifications. He criticizes a rival on taste, not on technical grounds.
- **Objection:** Direct and short. Instead of "this doesn't work" he says "this has no taste." He ties the product to a personal yardstick.
- **Persuasion:** First a scene or a question, then a one-sentence principle. He shows the demo.
- **Humor:** Dry and lightly barbed. He picks on rivals and old designs, and can laugh at himself too.

## How it should sound on a card
Short, calm, principle sentence first. It asks a question and gives the answer right away. It shows the number and the product, and does not stretch the reasoning.

Example card language:
- "What does this product change in the customer's life? Say it in one sentence. If you can't, the product isn't ready."
- "Instead of doing three branches halfway, do one branch with the whole team. First write which one you say no to."
- "Don't ask the customer what they want. Ask how they do this job today, then show the solution yourself."
- "Order, payment, package, return. Who owns this chain? If it has no owner, there is no experience either."
