# Steve Jobs: frameworks

## 1. The No Sentence (focus is about saying no)
Focus is not saying yes to one thing; it is saying no to many things that look good. In 1997 Jobs applied this by going from a scattered lineup to four big products (S2, S14). In 2004 he said the same principle: "it comes from saying no to 1,000 things to make sure we don't get on the wrong track or try to do too much" (S11).
1. Next to the new idea, write the sentence "if we say yes to this, which three things do we say no to."
2. List what you said no to so the team can see it.
3. Reopen the list after three months; ask for new evidence before removing a no.
Example: if a second branch, a new product line and marketplace sales were all proposed in the same quarter, say yes to only one.

## 2. The Two-Axis Grid (2x2)
In one meeting Jobs drew four squares on the board: Consumer and Pro across the top, Desktop and Portable down the side. The task was to make one great product for each square (S4, S14).
1. Pick the two axes you will split your customers on (for whom, which channel; individual/corporate, store/online).
2. Place your current products in the cells.
3. If a cell has more than one product, drop one; if a cell is empty, look at the price gap.
4. Write an owner for each cell.

## 3. The Triangle: Technology, Humanity, Design
According to Jobs, technology alone is not enough; married to the liberal arts and the humanities it gives the result that makes our hearts sing (summary, S13). The typography of the Mac is an example of this chain (S1).
1. Ask the product three questions: does it work (technology), is it good for people (humanity), is it beautiful and clear (design).
2. If one of the three is weak, delay the launch until that gap is closed.

## 4. Working Backwards from the Experience
"You've got to start with the customer experience and work backwards to the technology." (S2)
1. Write the customer's sentence at the moment of delivery: "I got this thing and now I can do this."
2. Draw the shortest steps backwards that make this sentence possible.
3. Remove every step that does not contribute to the sentence (feature, process, form field).
Example: in online sales, the sentence "I placed the order in 2 minutes, it was at my door the next day" shows which payment and shipping decisions it requires.

## 5. Listen but Don't Collect Requests
Jobs does not ignore the customer; he listens when the customer describes the job, not when they describe a solution. "People don't know what they want until you show it to them." (S10); "You can't just ask customers what they want and then try to give that to them." (S12)
1. Instead of "what do you want," ask the customer "how do you do this job right now, where do you get stuck."
2. Pick the friction that repeats across three customers.
3. Show the solution as a prototype and measure the reaction.

## 6. End-to-End Ownership
"People who are really serious about software should make their own hardware." (S8, quoted from Alan Kay). Jobs also says "I've always wanted to own and control the primary technology in everything we do." (S11).
1. Draw the experience chain (discovery, order, payment, package, delivery, return).
2. Write the owner of each link.
3. If the link customers complain about most is in someone else's hands, bring it in-house or replace that partner.

## 7. The A Team Rule
When the number of products drops, "we can put an A team on every single one of them" and the cycle goes from 18 months to 9 (S14). The lesson he drew from the Mac experience: A players only work with A players (summary, S4).
1. Write the number of active products.
2. Write which product gets your best three people; if there is no A person for every product, cut the number of products.

## 8. Speak with Values (Think Different)
"To me, marketing is about values." (S3) Nike sells shoes but in its ad honors athletes and athletics (summary, S3). "This is a very complicated world, it's a very noisy world." (S3) People don't remember much about you.
1. Tie the brand in one sentence to what it exists for.
2. Take feature counting out of ad and storefront copy.
3. Pull any product that contradicts this sentence from the line.

## 9. The Ambush and the Store Question
"Our goal in retail was not just to sell to the 5 percent of people who own our products today." (S15) Jobs overpaid for high-traffic locations.
1. When a branch is proposed, ask: does this location multiply my existing customers or reach new people?
2. Pick the location that reaches new people.
3. Write the person who will explain the product in the store (the Genius idea) next to the rent budget.

## 10. Price and Margin Discipline (after 1997)
Apple lost 1.05 billion dollars in 1997; in 1998 it posted a profit of 309 million dollars (S17). The line went from 15 platforms to 4 cells, the iMac sold at 1,299 dollars; Apple had no impressive product under 2,000 dollars (S14, S19).
1. Once the line is simpler, put a target price band on every cell.
2. Measure stock days and cut the item that waits longest.
3. Calculate margin on the simplified line; margin calculated on a scattered line misleads.
