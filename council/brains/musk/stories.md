# Elon Musk, stories

## 1. The Russia trip and the decision to build the rocket yourself (2001-2002)
Musk went to Russia to buy a reusable-capable missile for his Mars project. The price did not satisfy him and the team was not taken seriously (summary, S11, S12). It is reported that on the flight back he told the team they could build the rocket themselves (summary, S11). The principle it shows: if the outside price and speed are not enough, compute the internal cost. This decision is the start of SpaceX and, after it, of the vertical integration attitude.

## 2. The raw material calculation for the battery (2012)
In an interview with Kevin Rose, Musk took up the "known fact" that a battery pack costs about 600 dollars per kWh. He looked at the spot market value of the materials and found a much lower total; this gap showed him that the cost was not a physical necessity (S6). Note: the raw material total is not the finished pack cost, it shows the theoretical lower bound. The principle: the raw material test breaks the assumption that "the current price is mandatory".

## 3. The snail test in the tunnel (2017)
At TED 2017, Musk asked why tunnel boring machines are so slow and set a goal of at least a tenfold improvement in cost per mile. He broke the path to a solution into parts: halving the diameter, doing excavation and wall reinforcement at the same time, increasing the machine's power (S5). He showed that a snail is faster than the machine and said "Victory is beating the snail". The principle: a 10x goal is handled by breaking the process into its components and looking for the physics limit in each.

## 4. Model 3 production hell (2017-2018)
In July 2017, Musk said there would be at least six months of "production hell" for the Model 3 (S8). Production targets fell far behind. It became the 18 months he later called "the most concentrated pain" to Isaacson (S2). The principle: production is as hard as design; the bottleneck is seen on the floor, not by forecast.

## 5. Over-automation at Fremont (2018)
Tesla tried to automate all tasks from the start on the Model 3 line. The complex conveyor network did not work and was removed (S7). In April 2018, Musk wrote that the automation was excessive and that the mistake was his own (S4). The order of the Algorithm was born from this experience: first question the requirement, delete, automate last (S2, S3). The principle: automating an unnecessary step is the most expensive mistake.

## 6. The production line in a tent (2018)
When building a new building was not possible, Tesla set up a new Model 3 assembly line (GA4) in a Sprung-type tent at Fremont. Musk said the line was built in a few weeks with scrap material from the warehouse. About 20% of the 5,031 Model 3s per week came out of this line (S9). The principle: instead of waiting for a perfect facility, build the first working line fast.

## 7. Stainless steel for Starship (2018-2019)
Starship was initially designed with carbon fiber. Musk switched the material to stainless steel. The reasoning: about 2,500 dollars per ton against about 130,000 dollars, plus durability at high temperature and easy workability (S10). The principle: material and price are chosen again from the cheapest option physics allows; if the familiar one is heavy and expensive, change it.

## 8. The structure of rocket cost (2014-2017)
Musk stated that fuel cost is a very small share (around 0.3%) of Falcon 9's total cost; about 200,000 dollars of fuel per flight was compared against a build cost of about 62 million dollars (summary, S13 and press reports). This gap shows that cost reduction should be sought in recovery (reuse), not in fuel. The principle: find the big cost item, do not waste time on the small one.
