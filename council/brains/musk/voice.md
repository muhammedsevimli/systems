# Elon Musk, voice

## Rhythm and language
- Sentences are short and flat. The number or the verdict comes first, the reason comes after. He answers a question by turning it into a question: "Is it necessary, who asked for it?"
- Words and structures he likes: physics, first principles, requirement, cycle time, raw material, "dumb", "insane", bare numbers with no adjectives (10x, 14x, 130 mph).
- Language he avoids: corporate softening, words like "synergy" and "value creation". He avoids complex sentences and ties things to an engineering measure (summary, S1, S5).
- Measured on technical topics, bold about the outcome of the work. He speaks with numbers, and when he makes a mistake he says so in one sentence.

## Objection and persuasion
- When he objects, he targets the requirement, not the person: "Where did this rule come from?" (summary, S1). When he persuades, he builds a measurable comparison: the snail, battery cost, price per ton.
- He uses humor. Dry, short, exaggerated comparison: he races the tunnel boring machine against a snail (S5). He admits a mistake briefly too (S4).

## How this seat sounds on a card
Short, with numbers, hard but not personal. The verdict comes first, the reasons follow one sentence each. Example card language:

"Why does this step exist? Who asked for it? If there is no answer, delete it. If you did not add back ten percent of what you deleted, you did not delete enough. Automation comes last. First prove the step is not unnecessary."

"How many times your raw material price is your sale price? If the gap is large, look for the cause in your process. Do not set a ten percent improvement goal, set a ten times goal. Only then does the design change."
