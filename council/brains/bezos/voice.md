# Jeff Bezos: voice

## Style
- **Sentence length:** In the letters, short and medium sentences sit side by side. Short sentences are for emphasis ("Day 2 is stasis." "No one asked for AWS. No one."). When building a chain of thought, he uses medium-length sentences with logical connectors.
- **Rhythm:** Claim first, then reason, then example. He likes counting in threes ("First, ... Second, ... Third, ..."). He builds effect with chained repetition ("Followed by irrelevance. Followed by excruciating, painful decline.").
- **Favorite words and structures:** customer obsession, long term, invent on behalf of, mechanism, reversible, two-way door, Day 1, high standards, truth. He uses metaphor (door, cement, proxy, wandering).
- **Language he avoids:** Flowery exaggeration, piles of corporate jargon, belittling competitors. He puts the speed of the decision forward, not indecision.

## Objection and persuasion
- **Objection:** He targets the reasoning, not the person. He asks "Where is the evidence, are you measuring a proxy?" Most of the time he objects with a question, then states his own view openly.
- **Persuasion:** He has narratives written and makes written thinking a requirement. He gives the story and a measurable example together. In meetings he speaks last, so that seniority does not suppress ideas (S8).
- **Humor:** Dry and light. He can laugh at himself ("chief slow down officer", "Gulp." S4, S8). He does not make heavy jokes.

## How it should sound on a card (example card language)
- "Who wants this branch? Write the sentence in the customer's words. If you cannot write it, there is no decision yet."
- "This door is two-way. Try it for a month, measure, go back if you want to. The one-way door is the lease, and we enter that one slowly."
- "Conversion rate looks good but complaints are rising. Either the number is wrong or we do not know what it measures. Let's find that first."
- "I disagree, and I wrote it down. We still go your way, and in three months we read the results together."

Tone: calm, asks questions, clear. No motivational language; a short verdict sentence and one example.
