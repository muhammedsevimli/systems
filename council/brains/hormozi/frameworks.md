# Hormozi: frameworks

The frameworks are summarized from the books S1, S2 and S3. Examples with numbers are "test hypotheses": replace them with your own data. Every step is written with a "do the math" logic.

## 1. Value Equation
Value = (Dream outcome × Perceived likelihood of success) / (Time delay × Effort and sacrifice). Raise the first two, lower the last two (S1).
1. Score your offer from 1 to 10 on the four dimensions and find the lowest two scores.
2. If the denominator is weak: pull the first result from 30 days to 7 days, and do the customer's work yourself (done-for-you).
3. If the numerator is weak: add proof, case studies, a guarantee.
4. Present the change to 10 people and compare the "yes" rate.

## 2. Starving Crowd
Four signals when choosing a market: big pain, buying power, easy reach, growth (summary, S1).
1. Write the target audience in one sentence.
2. Answer yes/no on the four signals. If there are fewer than two yeses, change the market, not the offer.
3. Example: "Small boutiques in Istanbul": can they be found today in one place on Instagram, and do they have a budget? If not, the problem is not your price, it is your audience.

## 3. Grand Slam Offer
List the problems, find a solution and a delivery path for each problem, then trim and stack, and finally name it (summary, S1). Strengthen it with scope, bonus, guarantee and scarcity.
1. Write down 10 small obstacles the customer experiences.
2. One solution per obstacle; keep the ones with low cost and high perceived value.
3. Give the package a name that ties it to one outcome (target audience + outcome + time).
4. Write a currency value next to each component; if the total stays under 3 times the price, grow the package.

## 4. Guarantee types and risk reversal
Four types: unconditional, conditional, anti-guarantee (no refunds, and says so openly) and implied (summary, S1).
1. Tie the guarantee to something you can control (delivery, participation, time).
2. Calculate refund risk: if the refund rate is 10%, write the revenue and margin into the table.
3. If sales happen without a guarantee, don't add one; target the biggest objection.

## 5. Scarcity and urgency
Scarcity: limited capacity. Urgency: a deadline for the decision. Both must be real (summary, S1).
1. Calculate your capacity (e.g. I can take 8 customers a month).
2. Write the date and the number; when the deadline passes, actually remove the bonus.
3. If you notice it is fake, pull it from publication; losing trust is not cheap.

## 6. Theory of Constraints: lead, sales, delivery, profit
Solve one constraint (summary, S6; the theory of constraints is adapted from Goldratt).
1. Pull four numbers: weekly leads, close rate, monthly delivery capacity, gross margin.
2. Divide by the target customer count to find which step clogs first. Example: target of 20 sales a month, 200 leads, 5% close = 10 sales; the constraint is sales, not leads.
3. Run an experiment on that step only; don't touch the others.

## 7. Unit economics: LTV:CAC, 30-day cash and churn ceiling
LTV:CAC at least 3. Gross profit in the first 30 days pays back CAC (client-financed acquisition) (summary, S3).
1. LTV = monthly revenue × gross margin ÷ monthly churn. Example: 1,500 TL × 70% ÷ 8% = 13,125 TL.
2. CAC: ad + sales cost ÷ new customers. Example 3,000 TL; ratio 4.4 (passes).
3. 30-day test: first-month gross profit 1,050 TL < CAC 3,000 TL, so cash flow is open. Fix: raise the first payment (setup fee, annual prepayment, upsell) or lower CAC.
4. Target: first 30 days gross profit ≥ CAC. Verify from S3 whether there is a stricter multiplier (for example 2 times CAC); until then treat 1 times as the lower threshold.
5. Churn ceiling: customer ceiling = monthly new ÷ monthly churn. With 40 new customers a month and 8% churn the ceiling is 500; if churn drops to 4% the ceiling is 1,000. Don't raise the ad budget before churn drops (summary, S3, S6; arithmetic).

## 8. Courage to raise price
Value first, then price; a high price brings better customers and more referrals (summary, S1).
1. Write the current close rate and the gross profit per unit.
2. Calculate the break-even close rate: if the price goes from 1,000 to 1,250 TL, at a 60% margin the unit profit goes from 600 to 850; total profit increases unless the close rate falls below 21.2% from 30%.
3. Test for 4 weeks on a new customer group; protect existing customers.

## 9. Money Models: four offer roles
Attraction, Upsell, Downsell, Continuity (summary, S3).
1. Split one product into four roles.
2. In the entry offer, aim for cash flow, not for making money.
3. Use upsell and continuity to pass the 30-day profit threshold.

## 10. More, Better, New
Order of growth: first increase what already works, then improve it, and only last try a new channel (summary, S2).
1. Find the channel that works (e.g. a WhatsApp warm list).
2. Double the volume; if efficiency drops, move to the "better" stage.
3. "New" only when the first two are exhausted.
