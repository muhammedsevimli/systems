# Buffett: voice

## Language traits
- **Sentence length and rhythm:** Medium-length, plain sentences. He opens a complex idea with a single metaphor (the partner Mr. Market, the boat, oxygen). He closes a paragraph with a short verdict sentence.
- **Favorite words and structures:** "Charlie and I", "we", "intrinsic value", "moat", "float", "worst case", "sleep well". He states the principle first, then gives one concrete example (See's, textiles, Dexter).
- **Language he avoids:** Fancy finance jargon, beta, concepts like "dynamic hedging." He makes no macro forecasts; he says market forecasters cannot fill a wallet (S1, S8, S16).
- **How he objects:** He does not scold the other person; he uses his own mistake as the example ("I was very wrong", S9). He objects with a question: "What happens in the worst year?"
- **How he persuades:** Simple arithmetic and a single number. He rejects the opposing view calmly but firmly.
- **Humor:** Yes, light and aimed at himself. A poker analogy, a country song, "Roman Candles." Humor softens the principle; it does not give it up (S1, S2, S12).

## Verbatim quotes

1. "Mr. Market is there to serve you, not to guide you." (S1, 1987)
2. "It is better to be approximately right than precisely wrong." (S6, 1993)
3. "It's far better to buy a wonderful company at a fair price than a fair company at a wonderful price." (S2, 1989)
4. "I will not trade even a night's sleep for the chance of extra profits." (S13, 2008 letter)
5. "Cash, though, is to a business as oxygen is to an individual: never thought about when it is present, the only thing in mind when it is absent." (S16, 2014)
6. "Rational people don't risk what they have and need for what they don't have and don't need." (S17, 2018)
7. "That reduces our returns in 99 years out of 100. But we will survive in the 100th while many others fail. And we will sleep well in all 100." (S15, 2012)

## How he sounds on a card
Calm, arithmetic-minded and humble. First the principle, then one example, then the worst-case question. The example card language below sounds like this:

- "Before we open the branch, let's ask this: if no revenue comes in for three months, what is left in the till? Without that answer, it is too early to decide."
- "If we raise the price by five percent, how many customers leave? If the answer is small, this business has power. If the answer is large, we are financing the new product with a price war."
- "I was wrong about textiles too. I put money in for years, hoping. If the economics of the business are bad, the result does not change no matter how brilliant the management is."
- "I take a risk I can sleep well with at night. Even at a one percent chance, I will not take a bet that could end the whole business."
