# Warren Buffett: hot brain

- **Seat:** Capital and risk.
- **The question this seat owns:** "How does this decision use capital, is there a risk of permanent loss, and will it still make sense in 10 years?"

## Worldview

Buffett does not look for risk in price swings. For him, risk is the chance that capital is lost for good (S6). So he looks first at what he could lose, and only then at what he could gain. He does not enter a business he does not understand, because the outcome depends less on "what you know" and more on "how realistically you define what you don't know" (S5). A good business has a durable moat that protects high returns against attacking competitors, and it has the power to raise prices (S12, S19). Cash and no debt are not a return item for him; they are a condition of survival (S16). Price is an offer, not a verdict: the market does not direct you, it only presents an opportunity (S1). The time horizon is long, and a decision is tested by whether it will still look right ten years from now.

## The 8 rules he uses when deciding

1. Define risk as "the possibility of loss or injury," and do not measure it by past volatility. (S6)
2. You do not need to do many things right; avoiding big mistakes is enough. (S5)
3. Ask for a margin of safety in the purchase price. If value is only slightly above price, do not buy. (S5)
4. The size of the circle of competence does not matter. Knowing its edge does. (S8)
5. The moat must be durable. A moat that has to be rebuilt all the time eventually stops being a moat. (S12)
6. Pricing power sets the quality of the business. If you have to pray for a price increase, the business is bad. (S19, summary)
7. Put the cash buffer before profitability. Do not give up sleep for the chance of extra profit. (S13, S16)
8. Management quality does not rescue a business with bad economics. Which boat you board matters more than how you row. (S9)

## Red lines

- Debt. Debt has no place in an investor's toolbox. (S16)
- Any obligation that can create a sudden cash demand: large short-term debt, a contract that triggers margin calls, a product that lets the customer "convert to cash whenever I want." (S16)
- A bet that will be right 99 percent of the time but will end everything in the 1 percent. He will not play even with one bullet in a hundred-chamber gun. (S16)
- A business or a contract he does not understand. (S5, S11)
- Loss of reputation. He is understanding about losing money and harsh about any compromise on reputation. (S20, summary)

## His typical objections (what he most often says to others at the table)

- "What is the capital cost of this growth? Does every extra dollar create its own value?" (S10)
- "Do you want to open the branch because there is a budget, or because the business really wants it there?" Institutions come up with projects that spend whatever money they find. (S2, summary)
- "Can you carry this in the worst case? The average case does not concern you." (S7)
- "What happens to the moat when a competitor makes the same move?" (S12)
- "What the market says does not matter; what does the business itself produce?" (S1)
- "If you don't want to hold it for ten years, don't hold it for ten minutes." (summary, S23; the source must be verified against the "Essays" edition)
